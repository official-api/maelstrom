SOLID-PROPELLANT ROCKET MOTOR

A COTS solid-propellant grain with electrical squib ignition sits in a modular canister at MAELSTROM's aft section. It's a rocket, not a drone: the cost and weight budget goes into motor, nozzle, and warhead rather than batteries and flight electronics. This delivers a high-subsonic/transonic closing speed of 220-280 m/s (Mach 0.65-0.85), covering a 2km intercept in roughly 8 seconds — collapsing time-to-intercept well below propeller-driven interceptors. Model rendered directly from the thrust unit's CAD data, live cutaway.

ACTUATED CONTROL FINS

Four rectangular all-moving control fins on the Motor & Actuated Fins section provide 3-axis manoeuvring authority, steered by real-time corrections from the terminal guidance system toward the cluster's geometric centre. Miniaturised electromechanical servos give the round the tight pursuit curves needed to close on a fast-arriving, splitting drone cluster before it reaches a weapon-release line.

EO/IR TERMINAL GUIDANCE

A dual-band micro-camera feed is processed on onboard edge compute using DBSCAN clustering to fuse the whole drone cluster into a single aim point at its geometric centre, rather than locking onto one target. This prevents individual-target over-fixation and maximises multi-kill probability. The same sensor doubles as a proximity trigger, firing the hard-kill or soft-kill effector once in range.

ONE-TO-MANY KINETIC AREA EFFECTOR

Command-triggered or proximity-fused at the cluster's density centroid, the effector delivers controlled high-density fragmentation for hard-kill, covering a 10-15m radius. This achieves a 1:N engagement ratio — turning a raid of multiple drones into a single intercept instead of a one-for-one exchange, matching the economics of a swarm attack.
